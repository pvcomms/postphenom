The Center for Applied Postphenomenology

Four marks, each built from a concept the field already owns. Every one is a single ink on a single ground, so it survives a favicon, a letterhead and a footnote.

Direction A

The Rendered World

A circle is the world as given. A screen crosses it, and everything inside the screen is the same world re-rendered as dots: sampled, quantised, algorithmic. Half of what you see is real, half is the rendering, and the edge between them is the argument of the institute.

  • The favicon drops the bezel and keeps the split, which is what survives at 16 px.
Direction B

The Bracketed World

Husserl's epoché is bracketing: suspend the natural attitude and look at experience itself. Here the brackets are the technology. The world sits between two bezels and never leaves them. It is the most scholarly of the four, and the one that most looks like it belongs on a journal spine.

Direction C

The Relation

Don Ihde's notation for every human–technology relation is I → technology → world. Drawn straight, it is the institute's whole method in one line: a self, a screen, a world, and the arrows that now run through the screen whether we like it or not.

Direction D

Two Lives

A life in the world and a life on the feed, same size, overlapping. The solid circle is embodied life. The dotted circle is the mediated one. Where they overlap, the dots knock out of the ink: the digital life is eating into the first one, not sitting beside it.

Recommendation

Ship A as the mark and keep B as the typeable shorthand. A is the only one of the four that says "applied": there is a real screen in it, and the split between world and rendering is the sentence the whole center is about. It holds at 16 px, it works as a single cut of ink, and the cobalt version gives you one accent to spend on the site without touching the mark.

B lives in text: [ ● ] in email signatures, the footer, the Bluesky handle bio. C belongs inside the site as a diagram, not on the door. D is good but it is a genre.

Wordmark: Spectral, a serif drawn for reading on screens, set with the descriptor line in Plex Mono. "Postphenomenology" as one word, which is how the field writes it.
