Circuits

A method is a sequence of instruments, not one of them, and every call along it is yours. Three circuits, drawn; the links built from your own words.

Set one thing down here, in your words, and carry it from station to station. To evaluate a claim: provenance, sieve, muster, tack. To make a decision: bearing, overview, fence, act, spine. To hold a belief: course, canon, panel. Each box is a link that takes the words with it, and nothing else.

If you want a line to try before your own: The Thursday review can go now that the team is four. Take it or leave it; what you type below is the only thing that travels.

circuit one

Evaluate a claim

For a claim that reached you: where it came from, which worlds it could have come out of, what a room makes of it, and which of your own claims it moves.

circuit two

Make a decision

For a choice in front of you: where it sits among your values, how big the step is against the life it opens, what fence stands in the way, what you did about it, and how it went.

circuit three

Hold a belief

For something you already think: which way each thing that hit it bent it, how often you have reached for it, and how its markers read over time.

    what it leaves to you

    Every call. Whether the thing is a claim, a decision or a belief, and so which circuit it belongs in, if any, is yours to say; the page lists three and puts none above another. The order inside a circuit is a sequence, not a ranking: nothing here says one instrument is worth more than the next, or that the thing needs all of them, or that it needs any.

    What you did at a station stays at that station, in that instrument's own key. This page reads none of them and cannot tell whether you went. The only thing it carries is the words you typed here, by the address bar, verbatim; it goes nowhere else.
