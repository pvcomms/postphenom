    The claim

    Going round in circles is staying at the one angle you keep choosing. A card you did not choose, taken literally, puts you somewhere you would not have stood, and what you see from there is yours to say. The instrument picks no card over another and never says what one means.

    The starter deck is fifty-six prompts in this page's own words, dealt in a seeded shuffle so that nothing repeats until the pool is out. Your own cards go into decks beside it. Strike any deck and it leaves the shuffle until you put it back.

    The form is borrowed from Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt's Oblique Strategies (1975), and that deck is theirs. If you own it, type or paste the cards you want into a deck of your own here.

    It counts what is in the shuffle, what was dealt this sitting and how many notes you have kept. It never weighs a card, never picks one for you, and never says what one means. Your decks and notes are kept in this browser and go nowhere else; the sitting's draws are forgotten on reload.
