Most of a life is the middle. A stretch of days, each marked on a scale you name, falls into a shape, and the shape has a mean and a spread that are yours alone.

You write a stretch of days, a line each, and mark every one on a scale whose two ends you name. The page fits a normal curve to your marks and nothing else, draws the days as dots under it, shades the middle band, and counts what fell where. You say what you make of the shape; later you say how it went. It opens on a specimen, one month from a life that never happened. Make your own, and what you set down stays in this browser.

    what normal holds to

    You write the days and you mark them, on a scale whose ends are your words. The page takes the mean and the spread of your marks, and nothing else, fits the curve, draws each day as a dot under it, shades the band one spread either side of your mean, and counts the days in the band and in each tail. The counts are counts of your own entries against your own mean.

    It never scores the stretch or the life, never compares your shape to anyone else's, never says a tail is a problem or a middle is a failure, and never recommends. The set-down line is never prefilled except by your own words carried from another instrument, verbatim. No host is called; nothing is fetched; no model is asked; nothing you did not write is kept.
