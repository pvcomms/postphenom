Attention

Attention is something you compose, not something that is captured from you. A week set down before it is lived, and read against the week that was.

You write what will have your attention this week, or today, and give each thing its share: hours, or parts of a hundred. You mark each one as chosen, or as something that arrives unasked, the inbox, the feed. The page draws the composition to scale, so the shape of the week you meant is there before it is lived. At the end of the span you say what each thing actually had, and the drawing sets meant against had. It opens on a specimen, one week from a desk that never existed. Make your own, and what you set down stays in this browser.

      what attention holds to

      You name what will have your attention, give each thing a share of the span, and mark it chosen or arriving. The page dates the sheet, adds the shares, draws them to scale against the whole span, and says what is there: how many things, how much chosen and how much arriving, how much of the span is unassigned, and whether the shares add up. At the end you say what each thing actually had, and the drawing sets that beside what you meant, hollow wherever you did not say.

      It never ranks the things, never says what to cut, never says whether a week was good, never reminds you, and keeps no streak. Nothing is timed and nothing is tracked; every number on the page is one you typed. No host is called, nothing is fetched, no model is asked.
