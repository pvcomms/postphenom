Break

When it hurts: notice it, remember you are not the only one, be kind. Three sentences in your words, a hand where it helps, and what you would say to a friend in your spot.

Mindful self-compassion as an instrument, after Kristin Neff and Chris Germer. The three sentences are enough. Everything under them is there if you want it.

The claim

When something hurts and the critic has the floor, the voice you use on yourself is not the one you would use on a friend in exactly your spot, and from inside you cannot hear the difference.

So the page sets the two side by side and counts what each leans on, word by word: absolutes, shoulds, labels, contempt, you turned on yourself, and allowance. Every word it counted is shown so you can check it. It says what the friend's version drops or adds, and never which voice is right. The record counts breaks, hands, and which sentence was hardest to say. It never says you are hard on yourself, and it never scores.

It opens on a specimen: one of two breaks a made-up person took, with the friend's version and afterwards filled in. Make your own takes a break of yours. What you write stays in this browser and nowhere else.

      what the break holds to

      How it works: the three sentences are enough; everything under them is optional. The hurt is not a problem to be solved here. The friend's version is set beside yours and counted, word by word, every counted word shown, so you can see the difference. The record counts breaks and hands and which sentence was hardest, and dates each one.

      What it never does: say which voice is right; say you are hard on yourself; score a break or a record; propose a sentence, a hand or a word; call a host, fetch anything or ask a model; keep anything you did not write.
