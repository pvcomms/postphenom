Margin

What you said to yourself while you were looking. The margin is the tab at the edge, or the apostrophe, and this is where it lands: when, where, about what, in your words.

  • 'opens the margin, unless you are typing
  • ⌘↵ · ctrl↵keeps the note
  • esccloses it, or lets an edit go
  • double-clickchanges the words of a note you made
The claim

What you say to yourself while you look is part of what you saw, and it goes as quickly as it came. Get it out of your head in the words it arrives in. Nothing is asked of it yet.

Read back later, by day, by where you were, by what it was about or by a single word, the asides show you what you kept coming back to. The page counts them and says what it counted. It never summarises you.

It opens on a specimen: five asides a made-up person left while looking at their own notes. Two of them were spoken; this page keeps written notes only. What you write stays in this browser and nowhere else.

    what the margin holds to

    You write every note, in the words it came in. The page dates it, keeps it with where you were and what it was about, groups the notes by day, counts them by place and by thing, and reads the counts back. You change the words or take a note back; what any of it meant is yours to say, later and elsewhere.

    It never summarises you, never says which note mattered, never draws a thread between two notes you did not draw, never prefills a note except with your own words carried from another instrument, and never sends a word of it anywhere: no host is called, nothing is fetched, no model is asked.
