Way

Say where you are. Say where you mean to be, as it is and not as it will be. Keep the steps you would actually take. Then stand at the then and look back.

            what the way holds to

            how it is read: the then is written as if it is already so. that is the whole trick; imagining an outcome as having happened makes its causes easier to name, and the desk counts the sentences that still look ahead. the obstacles are the part that turns a picture into effort, each with a when–then. standing at the then is a mode, not a home: stay a minute, then come back to the steps.

            what it never does: propose a step, an obstacle or a change; say whether the way is a good one or whether you will walk it; put one step above another; call a host, fetch anything, or ask a model; keep anything you did not write.
